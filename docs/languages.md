# Languages

Per-language config, readings, scripts, right-to-left text, and local recordings.

- `src/languages/`: per-language config (`fr`, `de`, `ja`, `zh`, `ko`, `hi`, `he`, `ar`, `cs`, `es`, `pt`, `ru`, `it`, `gd`; picked in the popup, `Settings.language`, and changing it weaves the page again). `writingNotes` (set for `ja`, `zh`, `ko`, `hi`, `he`, `ar` and `ru`, for `gd`, to keep Irish out, and for `pt`, to keep European Portuguese out) goes into every prompt that writes the language (`writingNotesFor`): Japanese is written with readings, `日本語《にほんご》`, Chinese in simplified characters with pinyin on every word, `学生《xuéshēng》` (tone marks, citation tones; titles in “”, since `《》` are readings), Korean in Hangul with a Revised Romanization, as said, on every word with its particles, `학교에《hakgyoe》` (polite 해요체; a number in digits takes `｜3시《se si》`), Hindi with a romanisation on every Devanagari word, `घर《ghar》` (textbook diacritics), and Hebrew unpointed with an Israeli romanisation on every word, `בבית《babáyit》` (stress marked off the last syllable; the reader addressed in the masculine), Arabic (Modern Standard) unvoweled with a textbook romanisation on every word, `بالبيت《bil-bayt》` (read without case endings; the reader addressed in the masculine; voice `ar-XA`, `speechLocale`), and Russian in Cyrillic without stress marks, with a romanisation on every word, stress marked, `книга《kníga》`, which `src/ruby.ts` shows as `<ruby>` and drops for speech, comparisons and checks; text without `《` passes through unchanged. Japanese words also get romaji under them, written by code (`src/romaji.ts`: Hepburn from the kana and readings, words from `Intl.Segmenter` glued back with particle rules), shown by default at the levels in `LanguageConfig.romajiLevels` (A1, A2). A language with a romanisation (`LanguageConfig.romanSample`) gets a switch in the widget's title bar: romanisation over the script, under it, script only or romanisation only (`ScriptView`, kept per language in `Settings.scriptViews`, defaults in `scriptViewFor`), set as `data-tissage-script` on `<html>` so the page, widget, marks and popup follow it without redrawing (`scriptStyles` goes in every root that draws `rubyNodes`). The model leaves readings out of text it quotes in English (the note, English lines), so `fillPieceReadings` copies them in from the piece and the kit's words. `tests/ruby.test.ts` checks every word in a language with readings has one, across its syllabus, grammar points and word lists. Answers ignore Latin accents, Devanagari nukta and chandrabindu, Hebrew vowel points, and Arabic vowel marks and hamza seats (`sameAnswer`). Right-to-left text (Hebrew, Arabic) is found by its script, not its language (`src/direction.ts`, `setDirection`): an element mostly in such a script gets `dir="rtl"` (lines align right, inline words are isolated from the English around them, Practice tiles run right to left), and goes left to right again when only the romanisation shows. Scottish Gaelic has no Google voice and Whisper doesn't know it (`noVoice`, `notHeard`): no listening or speaking exercises. Adding a language is a file here, its word lists (`src/lessons/words/<code>.ts`, keyed in `easyWordsFor` and `a1WordsFor`), grammar points and syllabus; weave lessons are built per language (`lessonsFor(lang)` in `src/lessons/`)
- `src/devanagari.ts`: Hindi's romanisation, written by code for every Devanagari word `src/ruby.ts` shows (spans, `readingsOnly`, `readingsInBrackets`). The letters come from the script, in the scheme `hi.ts`'s writing notes give the model; which inherent a is said comes from the model's reading where it spells the same word (rules where it doesn't), and a word-final one always from a rule. A word keeps its first reading while the page is open. The prompts still ask the model for readings: they decide the inherent a, and the base is what's matched.
- `src/recordings.ts`: word recordings kept locally, never committed or zipped: `local/recordings/<lang>/` (gitignored: clips and an `index.json` keyed by `recordingKey`), copied into the unpacked build by `wxt.config.ts`. Text with a recording (a word, phrase or sentence, matched whole) plays it, at no cost (`runSpeech` in `background.ts`); a `noVoice` language gets a speaker that reads only what's recorded (`Speaker.can`), so 🔊 shows only on recorded words, passage lines and Practice items, with no Listen button and no listening exercises
